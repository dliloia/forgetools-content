# How to Build Trust With Your Team When It Has Not Been Earned Yet

A management title gives you authority. It does not give you your team's trust. People learn whether they can rely on you through the small decisions you make repeatedly: whether you show up, keep commitments, tell the truth, and respond fairly when something goes wrong.

## Make your behavior predictable

Keep regular one on ones when you can. If you need to cancel, explain why and reschedule. When an employee raises a concern, remember it and tell them what happened next. If you do not know the answer, say so and tell them when you will check again.

A tracking system is optional. A reliable habit is not. Write down commitments, review them, and close the loop. If you missed a deadline, own it directly instead of hoping the person forgets.

## Be honest about decisions and limits

Teams do not need a manager to have every answer. They do need to know when a decision is final, what context can be shared, and what remains uncertain. Do not dress up a constraint as an opportunity or promise a change you cannot deliver.

You may not be able to disclose confidential information. Say that plainly and share what you can. Honesty includes boundaries.

## Give credit and handle mistakes fairly

When the team does good work, name the people who did it. When something goes wrong, examine the facts before assigning blame. Protecting your team does not mean shielding anyone from accountability. It means being fair and taking responsibility for your own part.

Apply expectations consistently. If the same behavior is treated differently depending on who did it or what mood you are in, people will spend energy trying to read you instead of doing the work.

## Use one on ones to maintain trust

A recurring conversation is a practical place to follow up, listen, discuss feedback, and surface problems early. The meeting itself does not create trust. Your behavior in it does.

Ask what the employee needs from you. Listen without rushing to defend yourself. If you agree to act, do it. If you cannot, explain the reason and discuss another option.

## Start with a repair you can make now

Look at the commitments you have left open. Send the update someone is waiting for. Reschedule a meeting you canceled. Have the direct conversation you have been avoiding.

If you cannot complete a commitment by the date you gave, tell the person before they have to chase you. Explain what changed, offer a new date you can meet, and follow through. A missed promise can be repaired more easily when you address it directly.

Trust takes repeated evidence. One good speech cannot replace it, and one mistake does not have to define the relationship. Show the team what they can expect from you, then keep showing up that way.
