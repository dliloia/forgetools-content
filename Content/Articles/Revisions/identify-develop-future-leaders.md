# How to Identify and Develop Future Leaders on Your Team

Do not wait for someone to look fully ready before giving them a chance to grow. Look for evidence in how they handle responsibility, work with others, and respond to feedback. Then offer a stretch assignment with support and clear expectations.

Potential is not always loud. Someone who speaks confidently in meetings is not automatically prepared to lead. Nor should one mistake erase a person's chance to develop. Look at patterns and give people a fair opportunity to demonstrate what they can do.

## Look for behaviors that matter in the next role

Notice whether an employee brings context with a problem, asks questions about work beyond their own tasks, and takes responsibility for the effect their decisions have on others. Pay attention to how they handle disagreement and whether they share credit.

These signals are clues, not a verdict. Ask the employee what kind of responsibility they want. A strong individual contributor may not want to manage people, and that is a valid career direction.

## Design a stretch assignment carefully

Start with a skill the employee wants to build and a real business need. Choose work that is challenging but scoped. Define what success looks like, what decisions they can make, and when you will check in.

A stretch assignment should not be a way to give someone extra work without authority or recognition. Provide the context and access they need. Stay available to coach, but do not take over the assignment when the first obstacle appears.

For a hypothetical example, an employee interested in project leadership could own a small cross team launch. Agree on the scope, decision rights, and a check in each week. After the launch, discuss how they handled dependencies and what support they need for a larger assignment.

Afterward, discuss what went well, what was difficult, and what they want to try next. The debrief is part of the development, not an optional extra.

## Make opportunity visible and fair

People cannot demonstrate skills if they never get the opportunity. Share stretch work and leadership tasks transparently where possible. Be explicit about how people are selected, and review whether the same employees always get the visible assignments.

When someone does strong work, describe their contribution to decision makers. Do not rely on vague praise. Give examples of the scope they handled and the judgment they showed. If they are not ready for a role yet, explain what evidence is missing and how they can build it.

## Start with one person and one next step

Ask an employee what responsibility they would like to grow into. Agree on a project or task that gives them a chance to practice, then set a check in date. Growth takes work from both of you. Your job is to make the opportunity real and the expectations clear.
