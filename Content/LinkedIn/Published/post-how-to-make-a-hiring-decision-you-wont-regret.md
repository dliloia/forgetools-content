Hiring mistakes rarely happen in the interview. They happen in the debrief.

The room defaults to whoever speaks first, or whoever is most senior, or whoever tells the best story about their 45 minutes with the candidate. And then six months later you're having a very different kind of conversation about that person.

The single cheapest fix I know: every interviewer writes down their assessment before anyone talks. Not a paragraph, just a verdict and the observations behind it. Once you hear a colleague's read, your memory of the interview starts drifting toward theirs. Independent signal is fragile. Protect it before the group erases it.

The other thing most teams skip is building the rubric before they have a candidate in front of them. When someone impresses the room, the scorecard quietly reshapes itself around their strengths. You need to decide what the role actually requires before the first resume comes in, so you have something to hold the conversation accountable to.

A debrief should collect verdicts before it collects stories. That one shift changes everything about the quality of the decision you end up making.
