Most of my early jobs would embarrass a polished resume.

Hauling bread at 4am, digging trenches, cold-calling strangers. None of it looked impressive, but every context switch forced me to rebuild my map of how things worked. After enough reps, building the map became the skill itself. That's resourcefulness, and it's the one thing I've never worried about going obsolete.

The uncomfortable part is that most people don't stop growing because circumstances force them to. They stop because comfort is easier, and they find reasonable-sounding ways to justify it. Organizations don't help here either. They hold onto their best people, which sounds like a reward but often just means nobody is engineering your growth except you.

If you manage people, this applies to your team too. If your strongest performers are completely comfortable, you may be slowly managing them toward irrelevance, and your loyalty to them won't change that outcome.
