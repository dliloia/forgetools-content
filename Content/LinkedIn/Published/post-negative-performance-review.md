Most managers think the hard part is delivering the rating. It isn't.

The hard part is walking into that room knowing you softened things for six months, and Casey has no idea what's coming. That gap is yours to own before you say anything else. A brief, direct acknowledgment of it doesn't make you look weak. It makes the rest of the conversation land the way it needs to.

Once you've said the actual words, and I mean the actual words, the specific behaviors and the concrete examples, your job shifts. You're not there to make someone feel okay about hard news. You're there to make sure they leave with no confusion about where they stand and what the path forward looks like. Those are different goals, and conflating them is usually what makes these conversations worse.

The improvement plan matters as much as the conversation itself. Specific behaviors, a timeline, and honest clarity about what happens if nothing changes. That's not harsh. That's the job.
