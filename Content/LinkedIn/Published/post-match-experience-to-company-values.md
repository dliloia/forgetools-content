Most candidates read company values like they're studying for a multiple choice test.

The problem is the companies worth working for aren't giving multiple choice tests. They're looking for evidence that you've actually operated inside a principle under pressure, when the easier path was to ignore it. Nodding at the language isn't evidence. A specific story where you chose the harder thing is.

The part of this process that most people skip is the decoding step. Before you match your experience to a value, you have to understand what behavior the value actually rewards and, more usefully, what behavior it punishes. Those are two different questions and the second one tells you more about culture fit than anything on a careers page.

There's also a version of this that runs in reverse. If you go through your experience and realize you've spent years in environments that rewarded exactly what a company's values penalize, that's important information to have before you accept an offer, not after your first quarter.
