Spent three weeks fixing onboarding. The real problem was territorial senior reps.

That story is more common than most managers want to admit. A visible problem gets a plausible explanation, the explanation becomes a plan, and the plan gets executed before anyone asks whether the diagnosis was actually right. The result is a well-built solution to the wrong problem.

The pressure to act is real. Walking into a room with a problem and walking out with an action plan feels like leadership. Asking for more time to understand what's happening feels like hesitation, even when it's the smarter move. That tension pushes people toward speed, and speed usually means grabbing the most visible explanation available.

The most visible explanation is almost never the root cause. It's just the symptom that's loudest. The real cause is usually one or two more questions deeper, and those questions tend to point at something less convenient to fix.

Before you build the solution, write the root cause in one plain sentence and ask: what would have to be true for this to be wrong? That question alone would catch a lot of expensive mistakes before they happen.
