Most managers prepare what they'll say, not what they're carrying.

The distinction matters more than people realize. You walk into a hard conversation after weeks of building a case in your head, and the other person walks in cold. That gap alone explains most of the blow-ups. You're not starting a conversation at that point, you're delivering a verdict that the other person didn't know was coming.

The most useful prep work happens before you open your calendar. Get honest about what outcome you actually want, write down what's a verifiable fact versus what's a conclusion you drew, and think through where the other person genuinely is. Not where your frustration assumes they are.

And write your opening sentence. Read it out loud. If it sounds like you're bracing for impact, rewrite it until it sounds like you're trying to solve something together.
