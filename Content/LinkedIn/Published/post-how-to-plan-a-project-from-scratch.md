Most projects don't fail in execution. They fail in the ten minutes before kickoff.

Someone gets excited about an initiative, calls a meeting, hands out tasks, and assumes that momentum is the same thing as a plan. It isn't. A task list is what falls out of a real plan. The plan itself has to answer harder questions first: what does done actually look like, who owns what, and where are the dependencies that nobody's tracking yet.

The dependency piece gets skipped more than anything else. Teams jump straight to timelines, which means those timelines are built on assumptions disguised as facts. When things slip, it usually traces back to one moment where someone assumed a handoff would be fast, or an approval would be simple, or another team would move at the same speed. They didn't.

Building a plan backwards from the deadline, mapping dependencies before touching a Gantt chart, and putting one person's name on every deliverable, these aren't advanced techniques. They're just the basics that most managers never got taught because they learned project management by surviving projects.
