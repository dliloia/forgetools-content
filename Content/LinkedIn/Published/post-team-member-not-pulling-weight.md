Most managers wait too long, and their best people pay the price.

When someone on your team stops delivering, the instinct is often to give it more time, hope the pattern corrects itself, or avoid a conversation that feels uncomfortable. But delay isn't neutral. Every week you let it slide, you're sending a signal to the people who are showing up and doing the work that underperformance carries no real cost.

The first conversation matters enormously, and most managers either skip it or soften it into meaninglessness. Walk in with what you've observed directly, specific deliverables, specific dates, specific gaps between what you were told and what you saw. Leave out what teammates told you. That's not the conversation you're there to have.

Curiosity is the right posture going in. You don't always know what's behind the drop-off, and sometimes the person doesn't realize how visible the problem is. But being curious doesn't mean being vague. Morgan needs to leave that room knowing something has to change, not wondering if you were just checking in.
