Most diagnoses I've been part of ended with someone saying, "Yeah, we've known about that for a while."

Data and intuition aren't opposing philosophies. They're sequential tools, and most people skip the sequencing entirely. They either pull reports until they feel certain, or they act on gut feeling and call the data a formality.

The actual move is to write down your hypothesis before you touch any data. Your gut read will bias your interpretation if you don't surface it first. Name what you think is happening, then ask what the data would look like if you were right. Go find those signals. If they aren't there, you have a better question than you started with.

The part that gets skipped most often: talk to someone close to the work. Data shows you the shape of a problem. Intuition points you toward a cause. But the person running the process usually knows exactly what's wrong and hasn't been asked.
