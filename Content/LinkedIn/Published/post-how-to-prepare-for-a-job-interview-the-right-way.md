Most candidates spend an hour on the company website and call it prep.

The actual gap in interview performance almost never comes down to company research. It comes down to self-knowledge. Candidates who can't articulate what they need from a work environment, or who hedge every behavioral answer because they haven't actually thought through their own history, lose to people with weaker resumes who simply know themselves better. I watched this play out hundreds of times as an Amazon Bar Raiser.

Two things move the needle more than anything else: mapping your stories before you walk in, and getting clear on your own values before you try to pitch yourself against the company's. Both take real time. Neither is glamorous. Most people skip them.

Your questions for the interviewer also matter more than candidates realize. A sharp question signals you're already thinking like someone doing the job. A generic one confirms you're not.

The candidates who carry themselves as mutual evaluators, figuring out fit rather than seeking approval, are more confident, more specific, and genuinely more compelling to talk to. That posture is available to anyone willing to do the prep that makes it possible.
