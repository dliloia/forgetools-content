Most resilient people I know have the weirdest resumes.

That's not a coincidence. When you've had to adapt repeatedly, across different industries or roles or contexts, you build a reference library for hard situations. Problems that look catastrophic to someone with a narrow background just look like something you've handled a version of before.

Most hiring criteria still treats experience as time served. But tenure in a single context doesn't build range. It builds depth in one spot, which is genuinely valuable until conditions change and suddenly the map you spent 20 years drawing doesn't match the territory anymore.

The practical implication: take the sideways move sometimes. Own something outside your lane. Spend real time with customers, not polished briefings. Every uncomfortable context adds to what you can draw from when something genuinely hard arrives. Resilience isn't a personality trait. It's a library, and you build it by being in unfamiliar situations and coming out the other side.
