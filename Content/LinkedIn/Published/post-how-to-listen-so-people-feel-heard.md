Most people think they're decent listeners. They're usually just waiting politely.

There's a gap between hearing someone and making them feel heard, and most managers never close it because nobody told them what closing it actually requires. It's not a personality trait. It's a set of learnable mechanics.

The one I see managers skip most often: asking the second question. The first answer is almost always the safe answer. The second question, the one that follows up on what was unresolved or interesting in the first, is what opens the real conversation. "It's fine, just a little stressful" is not the end of a thread, it's the beginning of one.

The other thing worth saying: jumping to solutions before someone feels understood is one of the most reliable ways to signal you weren't really listening. Your solution lands on closed ground. The sequence matters more than the advice.
