The VP who looked most comfortable in the room told me he hated these things.

That moment stuck with me. He had twenty years of experience on me and still felt what I was feeling. He'd just gotten better at functioning through it, which is a very different thing than not feeling it at all.

Most people in uncomfortable professional settings are trying to prove they belong. That's the wrong game. Belonging is something you decide before you walk in, not something you earn by saying the right things to the right people. Someone invited you. That decision already got made.

The other thing worth sitting with: the room is too busy being nervous about itself to notice yours. Redirecting your attention toward being genuinely useful, asking real questions, listening closely, contributing when you have something actual to add, does more for your presence than any amount of internal confidence coaching.

This doesn't disappear with seniority. It just gets quieter the more evidence you accumulate that you've been in hard rooms before and survived them fine.
