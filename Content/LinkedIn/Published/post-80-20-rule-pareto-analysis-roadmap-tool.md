Most roadmaps are just negotiation outputs with a prettier name.

I've sat in planning sessions where every stakeholder walked in with their list, and the final roadmap reflected whoever had the most seniority or the most patience. That's not prioritization. That's a lobbying process with a deadline.

A Pareto analysis changes the room because it shifts the conversation from "my team needs this" to "here's what the data says is hurting customers most." You can still be overruled. But you've moved the discussion from opinion to evidence, and that difference compounds over time.

One thing people miss when they run this: frequency and dollar impact tell different stories, and you need both. The complaint that shows up twice might represent a churned customer worth forty thousand dollars. The one that shows up fifty times might be a minor annoyance. Sort by only one lens and you build the wrong roadmap.

The analysis itself takes half a day the first time. Spreadsheet, categories, impact estimates, rank descending. The hard part is the categorization, where real judgment lives, not the math.
